## 1. Study Objectives & Hypotheses

### 1.1 Primary Objective
To determine whether the computationally inferred coordinates generated by MSK-Therapy-OS achieve anatomical spatial concordance with expert-annotated anatomical landmarks within a prespecified clinical non-inferiority margin ($\delta_0 = 3.0\text{ mm}$).

### 1.2 Primary Hypotheses
For each anatomical cohort (A, B, C):
- **Null Hypothesis ($H_0$):** True population mean Euclidean radial error $\mu \ge 3.0\text{ mm}$.
- **Alternative Hypothesis ($H_1$):** True population mean Euclidean radial error $\mu < 3.0\text{ mm}$.

---

## 2. Prespecified Endpoints & Acceptance Thresholds

Evaluated exclusively on the **locked holdout partition (25%–30%)**:

1. **Primary Endpoint:** Mean Euclidean Radial Error across all evaluated landmarks $\le 3.0\text{ mm}$.
2. **Secondary Endpoint 1 (Dispersion):** Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) $\le 4.0\text{ mm}$.
3. **Secondary Endpoint 2 (Tail Risk):** 95th Percentile Radial Error (P95) $\le 6.0\text{ mm}$.
4. **Secondary Endpoint 3 (Gross Outliers):** Proportion of errors exceeding $10.0\text{ mm} \le 2.0\%$.
5. **Secondary Endpoint 4 (Confidence Interval):** Upper bound of cluster-bootstrap 95% CI $\text{RMSE}_{\text{upper}} \le 4.5\text{ mm}$.

---

## 3. Statistical Modeling & Estimation Procedures

### 3.1 Intra-Subject Clustering & Design Effect
Because multiple landmarks are measured on the same human subject, residual errors are clustered ($\rho \approx 0.35\text{–}0.40$).
- **Primary Estimator:** Linear Mixed-Effects Model (LMM) with subject-specific random intercepts:
  $$y_{ij} = \mu + u_i + \beta_{\text{site}} X_{\text{site}} + \epsilon_{ij}, \quad u_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_u^2), \quad \epsilon_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_e^2)$$
  where $y_{ij}$ is the Euclidean distance error for subject $i$ at landmark $j$.
- **Cluster Bootstrap Confidence Intervals:** 
  - Resampling unit: Subject ID with replacement.
  - Number of replications: $B = 1,000$.
  - Random seed: `20261030`.
  - Confidence limits: Empirical percentiles $\left[ P_{2.5}^*, P_{97.5}^* \right]$.

### 3.2 Site and Scanner Heterogeneity
- Multi-site data will incorporate random effects for `hospital_site_id` and fixed covariates for `slice_thickness_mm` and `field_strength_tesla`.

### 3.3 Multiplicity Control
- Primary cohort non-inferiority decisions are evaluated at one-sided $\alpha = 0.05$.
- Secondary point-by-point exploratory comparisons utilize Benjamini-Hochberg False Discovery Rate (FDR) control at $q = 0.05$.

### 3.4 Missing Data & Landmark Indeterminacy
- If a landmark is obscured due to metal artifact, post-surgical deformity, or anatomical variation:
  - Documented in protocol deviation log.
  - Imputation is **strictly prohibited**.
  - Analyzed under Missing at Random (MAR) assumption via full information maximum likelihood (FIML) within the mixed-effects framework.
  - If $>15\%$ of landmarks are unidentifiable for a given subject, the study is flagged for secondary sensitivity analysis excluding that subject.
