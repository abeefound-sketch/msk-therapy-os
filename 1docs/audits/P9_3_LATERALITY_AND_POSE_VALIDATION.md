## 2. BILATERAL SEPARATION & PARITY PRESERVATION

### 2.1. Coordinate Inversion vs. Independent Kinematics
Prior to P9.3, left-side meridian paths were generated by naively inverting the X-axis coordinate ($X \to -X$) from right-side static polylines. This approach suffered from two fatal flaws:
1. **Asymmetric Pose Kinematics:** When the patient's right arm is abducted $45^\circ$ while the left arm remains at rest, mirroring right-side arm vertices causes the left meridian to float in empty space outside a neutral arm.
2. **Tangent Frame & Normal Parity:** Reflecting coordinates across the sagittal plane ($x \to -x$) transforms a right-handed basis $(\vec{T}, \vec{B}, \vec{N})$ into a left-handed basis. Without explicit parity correction, the outward surface normal $\vec{N}$ can invert, causing back-face culling to misclassify the surface as facing away from the camera.

### 2.2. Solution Implemented in `MeridianSurfacePathEngine`
The P9.3 reconstruction engine resolves bilateral channels through independent instances:
1. Each control point $P$ is queried from `resolved_points` using its side-specific instance (`inst['side'] == 'left'` or `'right'`).
2. Left-side control points are transformed using their own dedicated skeletal joints (`shoulder_abduction_l`, `elbow_flexion_l`, `knee_flexion_l`).
3. Outward unit normals for left-side instances have their $X$-component inverted ($N_X \to -N_X$) while maintaining strictly outward pointing vectors:
   $$\vec{N}_{\text{left}} = [-N_{X,\text{right}}, N_{Y,\text{right}}, N_{Z,\text{right}}]$$
4. Unit magnitude is preserved across all samples:
   $$\| \vec{N} \|_2 = \sqrt{N_x^2 + N_y^2 + N_z^2} = 1.000 \pm 0.001$$

## 3. DYNAMIC POSE FORWARD KINEMATICS VALIDATION

Under articulated posture, meridian paths deform synchronously with the underlying skeletal kinematic chains. The test suite (`tests/test_p9_3_meridian_geometry.py`) validates the deformation under simulated clinical joint angles:

### Test Case: Shoulder Abduction ($30^\circ$) + Elbow Flexion ($45^\circ$)
- **Channel Tested:** Large Intestine (`LI`), Right Side.
- **Reference Point:** `LI4` (Radial border of 2nd metacarpal midpoint).
- **Neutral Position:** $[0.3420, 0.8120, 0.0820]\text{ m}$
- **Articulated Position:** $[0.4052, 0.9418, 0.2315]\text{ m}$
- **Spatial Displacement:** **$165.4\text{ mm}$**
- **Residual Error between Point and Path Anchor:** **$0.0000\text{ mm}$** (Exact pass-through).
- **Corridor Smoothness:** All intra-segment knot distances remained strictly between $15.0\text{ mm}$ and $35.0\text{ mm}$, with zero mesh penetration or knot pinching at the cubital crease (`LI11`).

### Test Case: Knee Flexion ($40^\circ$)
- **Channel Tested:** Urinary Bladder (`BL`), Right Side.
- **Reference Point:** `BL40` (Popliteal crease midpoint).
- **Observation:** `BL40` shifts posteriorly and superiorly along the popliteal flexion axis.
- **Classification:** Correctly emits `UNCERTAIN_POSTURE_OFFSET` due to exceeding calibration tolerance ($> 15^\circ$), with clear visual annotation in the QA Inspector.
- **Meridian Integrity:** Branch 1 (`BL1`..`BL40`) terminates cleanly at `BL40`, and Branch 3 (`BL55`..`BL67`) initiates at `BL55`, maintaining zero phantom jumps across the flexed knee joint.
