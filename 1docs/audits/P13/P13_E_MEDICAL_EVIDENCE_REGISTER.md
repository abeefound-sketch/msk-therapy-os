## 1. EVIDENCE CORPUS

The educational curriculum and interactive calculations in the MSK-Therapy-OS Shoulder Studio are mapped to classic and contemporary peer-reviewed musculoskeletal literature:

### EV-01: Scapulohumeral Rhythm Foundation
- **Citation:** Inman VT, Saunders JB, Abbott LC. *Observations on the function of the shoulder joint.* Journal of Bone and Joint Surgery (Am). 1944;26(1):1-30.
- **Biomechanical Finding:** Defined the foundational 2:1 overall ratio between glenohumeral elevation and scapulothoracic upward rotation during full thoracohumeral elevation ($120^\circ$ GH + $60^\circ$ ST = $180^\circ$ total).
- **Curriculum Mapping:** Module 4 & Rhythm Ratio Gauge.

---

### EV-02: Scapular Dyskinesis & Anterior Tilt in Impingement
- **Citation:** Ludewig PM, Cook TM. *Alterations in shoulder kinematics and associated muscle activity in people with symptoms of shoulder impingement.* Physical Therapy. 2000;80(3):276-291.
- **Biomechanical Finding:** Proved that symptomatic individuals exhibit statistically significant decreases in scapular upward rotation, increases in anterior tilt, and decreased serratus anterior activation during arm elevation.
- **Curriculum Mapping:** Module 3 & Module 4.

---

### EV-03: Pectoralis Minor Shortening & Scapular Tethering
- **Citation:** Borstad JD, Ludewig PM. *The effect of long versus short pectoralis minor resting length on scapular kinematics in healthy individuals.* Journal of Orthopaedic & Sports Physical Therapy. 2005;35(4):227-238.
- **Biomechanical Finding:** Individuals with a shortened pectoralis minor resting length demonstrate increased anterior scapular tilt and internal rotation during humeral elevation, establishing the physical tethering mechanism.
- **Curriculum Mapping:** Module 2 & Posture Severity Slider.

---

### EV-04: Muscle Imbalance & Upper Crossed Syndrome
- **Citation:** Kendall FP, McCreary EK, Provance PG, Rodgers MM, Romani WA. *Muscles: Testing and Function with Posture and Pain.* 5th ed. Baltimore: Lippincott Williams & Wilkins; 2005.
- **Biomechanical Finding:** Detailed the reciprocal length-tension imbalances in forward head/rounded shoulder postures: hypertonic pectoralis minor/major and upper trapezius vs. functionally inhibited lower trapezius, rhomboids, and deep cervical flexors.
- **Curriculum Mapping:** Module 1, Module 3, Module 5.

---

### EV-05: Janda's Reciprocal Inhibition & Sensorimotor Approach
- **Citation:** Page P, Frank CC, Lardner R. *Assessment and Treatment of Muscle Imbalance: The Janda Approach.* Champaign, IL: Human Kinetics; 2010.
- **Biomechanical Finding:** Emphasized that passive soft tissue mobilization alone fails to correct postural syndrome without targeted sensorimotor activation and neuromuscular re-education.
- **Curriculum Mapping:** Module 5 & Module 6 ("Why Massage Alone Is Incomplete").

---

### EV-06: Mechanoreceptive Effects of Manual Soft Tissue Release
- **Citation:** Schleip R. *Fascial plasticity – a new neurobiological explanation: Part 1 and 2.* Journal of Bodywork and Movement Therapies. 2003;7(1):11-19, 7(2):104-116.
- **Biomechanical Finding:** Manual manipulation activates low-threshold mechanoreceptors (Ruffini endings, interstitial receptors), leading to an immediate local parasympathetic shift and transient tone reduction, explaining the immediate subjective sensation of lightness.
- **Curriculum Mapping:** Module 1 ("The Core Paradox").
